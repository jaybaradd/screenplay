THE DEBT AT NOON

An original contemporary test screenplay after the dramatic premise of
Anton Chekhov's public-domain one-act play THE BEAR (1888).

    
INT. FARMHOUSE DINING ROOM - MORNING

Rain taps against two tall windows. The room is clean but dim. A long dining
table has been reduced to one place setting. On the wall, a clock marks every
second too loudly.

MARA VALE, 34, dressed in plain dark clothes, sits with a cup of coffee gone
cold. A framed photograph of her late husband, ADRIAN, faces her from the other
end of the table.

DEV HOLT, 63, the farm's caretaker, enters with a stack of unopened envelopes.
He places them beside Mara's cup, careful not to cover the photograph.

DEV
The post came early. There is another notice from the transport company.

MARA
Put it with the others.

DEV
The others have begun to lean.

Mara looks at the stack. It is, in fact, leaning.

MARA
Then put them in a drawer.

DEV
Drawers do not stop interest from growing.

MARA
Today is one year. I am not discussing accounts today.

Dev softens. He turns Adrian's photograph slightly, straightening it.

DEV
I know what day it is. The wheat does not.

Outside, an engine rattles, stops, then starts again before finally dying.

Dev looks through the window. A mud-streaked delivery truck stands at the end
of the drive. VIKTOR DANE, 39, climbs out holding a sealed brown envelope.

DEV
That will be the man from the notice.

MARA
Tell him I receive no visitors.

DEV
He does not look like a visitor.

The front gate CLANGS shut.


EXT. FARMHOUSE VERANDA - LATE MORNING

Viktor waits under the narrow roof, rainwater running from his coat. He keeps
the brown envelope dry against his chest. Dev stands in the doorway, blocking
the entrance without appearing to do so.

VIKTOR
Viktor Dane. Dane Transport. I need five minutes with the owner.

DEV
The owner is unavailable.

VIKTOR
Her signature appears on three letters saying the same thing.

DEV
Then you have already heard from her.

Viktor glances toward his truck. Through its open window, a mobile phone rings
on the dashboard. He lets it ring out.

VIKTOR
My drivers are expecting their wages by four. Her husband owed my company
twelve thousand for last season's haulage. I need an answer before the bank
closes.

DEV
Mr. Vale died before last season ended.

VIKTOR
The invoice did not.

Mara appears behind Dev.

MARA
You have your five minutes.

Dev steps aside. Viktor removes his wet boots without being asked and leaves
them neatly by the door. Mara notices the gesture but gives him no credit for
it.


INT. FARMHOUSE DINING ROOM - NOON

Viktor sets the brown envelope on the table. It contains a contract, three
invoices and a handwritten delivery log. Mara remains standing. Dev waits near
the kitchen door with a tray holding a teapot and three empty cups.

VIKTOR
Your husband asked me to extend the deadline twice. I did. Then your office
stopped answering.

MARA
My office was a hospital corridor.

VIKTOR
And mine is six people who worked those nights and have already waited.

Mara opens the farm ledger. Several pages have been cut out cleanly near the
back. She turns them one by one, unwilling to believe the gap.

MARA
These figures were not in the accounts I inherited.

VIKTOR
They were in the work your farm received.

MARA
Are you calling my husband dishonest in his own house?

VIKTOR
I am calling a debt unpaid. The dead do not become honest merely because we
miss them.

Dev puts the tray down harder than intended. The cups jump in their saucers.

DEV
There are boundaries, sir.

VIKTOR
There are also due dates.

Mara closes the ledger.

MARA
You will leave now.

VIKTOR
Not without a payment or collateral.

MARA
Take the north tractor.

DEV
Mara--

VIKTOR
It has a cracked axle. Your husband offered it to me in February.

Mara's anger falters. Viktor immediately regrets the satisfaction on his face,
but not enough to hide it.

MARA
Then take the grain drill.

VIKTOR
And stop your planting? That would give me a broken customer instead of an
overdue one.

MARA
How generous of you to protect me from ruin while demanding cash I do not have.

VIKTOR
How convenient to turn grief into a locked gate.

Silence. Rain drums against the windows.

Mara crosses to a small cabinet and unlocks it with a brass key from a chain at
her waist. Inside is an old target pistol in a wooden case. She removes the
case, places it on the table, and opens it. The pistol is dusty and unloaded.

Dev freezes with the teapot in his hand.

MARA
Adrian said every argument ended when this appeared.

VIKTOR
That may explain his accounting.

Mara looks from the pistol to Viktor. Then, unexpectedly, she laughs once. It
is not a happy sound, but it is the first laugh the room has heard in a year.

Viktor takes one careful step back.

VIKTOR
For the record, I prefer invoices.

Mara closes the case and pushes it away.

MARA
For the record, so do I.

She pulls the delivery log from the envelope and studies it closely.

MARA
This final load never reached the western storehouse.

VIKTOR
The river road flooded. Your husband redirected it to the old mill.

DEV
The mill was sold that same week.

The three of them understand at once: Adrian moved the grain without entering
the sale in the farm ledger.

Viktor gathers the invoices, but Mara keeps the delivery log under her hand.

MARA
This stays with me until I verify it.

VIKTOR
The original stays with me.

Their hands meet on the page. Neither releases it.

DEV
The tea is getting stronger while both of you become less reasonable.

The lights CUT OUT. The room goes still except for the rain.


INT. FARM OFFICE - EARLY AFTERNOON

Emergency daylight comes through a small window. Ledgers, seed catalogues and
repair manuals crowd the shelves. Dev lights a battery lantern and places it
between Mara and Viktor.

INA REED, 26, the farm's part-time accountant, hurries in carrying a laptop and
a canvas document bag. Her hair and shoulders are wet from the rain.

INA
I came as soon as Dev called. The network is down, but I downloaded last year's
bank statements yesterday.

Mara gives Ina the delivery log. Viktor keeps the brown envelope and invoices
beside his chair.

Ina compares dates, tapping figures into a calculator. No one speaks. The
calculator buttons sound almost as loud as the clock in the dining room.

INA
The transport invoices are legitimate. But there is also a deposit from the
mill two days after the redirected delivery.

MARA
Where did the money go?

INA
It left the account the same afternoon. Personal transfer. No description.

Mara sits back. For the first time, the anger leaves her face entirely.

MARA
He told me the farm was secure.

DEV
Perhaps he wanted it to be true before he said it.

Viktor reaches for the envelope, then stops.

VIKTOR
My problem is still real. But it is no longer only yours.

MARA
Do not mistake new information for forgiveness.

VIKTOR
I was speaking about the missing money.

MARA
So was I.

Ina suppresses a smile and turns the laptop toward them.

INA
The emergency reserve can cover one third today without stopping payroll or
planting. The balance could be tied to the first grain payment, if both parties
sign.

VIKTOR
My drivers cannot divide promises among themselves.

MARA
They would have one third of something instead of all of nothing.

Viktor studies her. His impatience has become reluctant respect.

VIKTOR
You negotiate better than your husband.

MARA
I keep better records.


EXT. MACHINERY SHED - LATE AFTERNOON

The rain has stopped. Water drips from the shed roof. Beyond the yard, the
fields shine under a pale strip of clearing sky.

Ina places a handwritten payment agreement on the hood of Viktor's truck. Mara
signs first. Viktor signs beneath her name. Ina photographs both pages with her
phone and returns the originals to the brown envelope.

Dev arrives carrying the same tea tray. Two cups are now filled. He gives one
to Mara and one to Viktor, keeping the third for himself.

DEV
This is the fourth pot. I refuse to let it become evidence.

Viktor takes a sip. It is painfully strong, but he manages not to react.

MARA
Your drivers will have the transfer before four.

VIKTOR
And I will return after the first grain payment.

MARA
For the balance.

VIKTOR
Of course.

He closes the brown envelope and places it inside his coat. Mara still holds
the copied delivery log.

Viktor walks to the driver's door, then looks back toward the farmhouse.

VIKTOR
You should open the curtains in that dining room.

MARA
You should repair your engine.

Viktor tries the ignition. The truck coughs but does not start.

Dev drinks his tea, pleased with the universe.

Mara allows herself a small smile. She takes the brass key from her waist and
hands it to Dev.

MARA
Put the pistol case in storage. Keep the ledgers on the table.

Dev closes his hand around the key.

Viktor tries the ignition again. The engine catches.

Mara watches the truck leave, then turns toward the open farmhouse door.

FADE OUT.
